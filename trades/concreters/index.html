The problem

Why concreters lose jobs online.

01

Work hidden in Instagram

Your best pours are buried in a feed full of reels and progress shots. Customers want to see finished driveways by type, and Google can't index your Instagram, so those searches end up with someone else.

02

Wrong-sized enquiries

Without clear service info, you get calls for tiny patch repairs when you'd rather be doing driveways and slabs. A site that shows the jobs you prefer filters people before they ever ring.

03

Finishes not explained

Homeowners don't know the difference between broom, exposed, stamped and polished. If your site doesn't explain it, they ask for a cheap plain slab, and you lose the chance to sell them a better finish.

What you get

What a concreter website needs. All of it, built in.

Gallery by finish type

Driveways, patios, slabs and decorative work sorted by finish, so a customer can tap exposed aggregate and see only that. Fast loading even with plenty of high-res photos.

Process and timeline

A simple page showing how a job runs: site visit, excavation, formwork, pour and curing. Answers the questions customers ask every time and makes you look organised.

Instagram feed connected

Your latest pours pulled straight onto the site, so it never looks stale. Post to Instagram as normal and the website quietly updates itself, no logins needed.

Questions concreters ask

Straight answers.

How does SEO work for a concreting website?

I write pages around searches like concrete driveway, exposed aggregate or house slab, combined with the suburbs, towns or counties you cover. The site loads fast, the schema marks you as a concrete contractor, and your Google Business Profile gets set up. Businesses I've built for have had first Google leads 45 days after going live, though every area is different.

What photos should I send?

Finished driveways and patios shot in good light, ideally just after sealing when the colour pops. Wide shots showing the whole job, plus close-ups of the finish. Pour day action shots with the crew are great too, and drone photos are a bonus. Send plenty and I'll pick the strongest.

Can I show both residential and commercial work?

Yes. I'll give each its own page so homeowners see driveways and patios, while builders and businesses see slabs, footpaths and larger pours. Each customer feels the site was made for them, and Google gets more specific pages to match to searches.

I mostly get work through Instagram. Do I still need a site?

Instagram is brilliant for showing work, but people searching Google for a concreter won't find your profile. A website catches those searches, holds your best work in one tidy place, and gives you a professional link to send. Your Instagram feed connects to the site, so the two work together.

How long does the build take?

About two weeks. Send me your Instagram on day one and you'll have a free working preview within 48 hours. Then we spend roughly ten days on your photos, services and areas while I write the words and set up your Google profile. On day 14 it goes live on your own domain.

The problem

Why hvac contractors lose jobs online.

01

Peak season chaos

When everyone's air con dies in the same week, you can't answer every call. Without a website that captures job details, those people try the next company and never come back to you for servicing later.

02

Quiet months, empty diary

Most HVAC sites only talk about repairs. They don't sell tune-ups, servicing or maintenance plans, so nothing pulls in work during the shoulder seasons, which is exactly when your team needs it most.

03

Big installs feel risky

A new system is a big spend. If your site doesn't explain the options or mention finance, people stall, collect five quotes and pick whoever made them feel most confident, which isn't always the best installer.

What you get

What a HVAC contractor website needs. All of it, built in.

Maintenance plan page

A page that sells your service plans clearly: what's included, how often you visit and why it prevents breakdowns. Builds a list of repeat customers who book in the quiet months.

Seasonal banners

Finance options explained

Questions hvac contractors ask

Straight answers.

How does SEO work for HVAC companies?

Heating and cooling searches change with the weather, so I build pages for both repair and install searches, plus your service areas. The site is fast, marked up with schema as an HVAC business, and your Google Business Profile is set up properly. Rankings vary with competition, but your pages will be ready before the next rush instead of after it.

What photos should I send?

Finished installs inside and outside the home, neat ductwork, outdoor units on clean pads, and commercial rooftop units if you do that work. Your vans and team in uniform help too. Avoid shots with clutter or messy cables in the background. Daylight phone photos are fine, and I'll crop and sort them for you.

Can I promote maintenance plans on the site?

Yes, and you should. I'll build a dedicated page explaining your plan, what's included and how to sign up, with a form that sends the details straight to you. It's one of the best ways to even out the seasonal swings and build a customer base that keeps coming back.

Should I mention finance on my website?

If you offer it, yes. Big system replacements often stall on price, and a clear finance section gives people a way forward. I'll explain the options in plain language and link to your provider. I won't make claims about approvals or rates, as that's between the customer and the lender.

What if I get more calls than I can handle in summer?

Good problem to have. The breakdown form lets people leave details so you can ring back in order of urgency. If you want calls answered while the whole team is out, my AI receptionist service can take job details and send them to you. That one is quoted separately.

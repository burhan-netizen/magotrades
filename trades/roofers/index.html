The problem

Why roofers lose jobs online.

01

Storm calls go elsewhere

After hail or high winds, people want someone on the roof today. If your site buries the phone number or takes ten seconds to load on mobile data, they back out and call the next roofer on the list.

02

No proof of the work

Homeowners can't climb up and check your past jobs. Without clear before and after photos of real roofs, you look the same as every cowboy with a ladder and a ute, and price becomes the only thing they compare.

03

Invisible for the money jobs

Re-roofs and full replacements are where the margin is. If you don't have a page that targets roof replacement in your area, Google sends those big-ticket searches to competitors who do.

Questions roofers ask

Straight answers.

How does SEO work for a roofing website?

I build each page around what people actually type, like roof leak repair or roof replacement plus your area. The code is fast and clean, the schema tells Google you're a roofing contractor, and your Google Business Profile gets set up properly. Some businesses I've built for saw first leads from Google 45 days after launch, but results vary by area and competition.

What photos should I send you?

Send your best finished roofs from street level and from up top, plus a few before and after pairs. Drone shots are gold if you have them. A photo of you and the crew on site helps too. Phone photos are fine as long as they're sharp and taken in daylight. I sort and crop everything.

Can the site cope with a surge after a big storm?

Yes. It's hand-coded and lightweight, so it stays quick even when lots of people hit it at once. I can also add a banner you switch on after a storm, telling people you're taking emergency call-outs and roughly how fast you can get there.

Do I need a separate page for insurance work?

If you do a fair bit of it, yes. People searching for storm damage help have different worries to someone pricing a new roof. A dedicated page answers their questions, explains how you deal with assessors, and gives Google a clear signal that you handle that work.

Is there a monthly fee?

No. It's a one-off build fee, and the local figure is on your country page. There's no monthly rent and no yearly fee, and you own the domain and the code. One full year of edits and support is included. After that you can leave it, pay hourly for changes, or update it yourself.

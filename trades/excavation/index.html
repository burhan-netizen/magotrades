The problem

Why excavation and earthmoving lose jobs online.

01

No machine list

Builders want to know if you run a five tonne excavator, a posi track or a tipper before they call. If your site doesn't say, they move on to someone whose fleet is listed clearly.

02

Tight access unclear

Plenty of backyard jobs need a small machine through a narrow gate. If you don't show your access widths and mini gear, those homeowners assume you're too big and call someone else.

03

Builders can't vet you

Commercial clients check insurance, inductions and past projects before booking a subbie. A social page full of machine selfies doesn't give them enough to put your name on their job.

What you get

What a excavation contractor website needs. All of it, built in.

Machine and attachment list

Every machine in your fleet with tonnage, width and attachments like buckets, augers, rippers and hammers. Clients know exactly what turns up and can book the right gear.

Tight-access section

A page showing your smallest machines, the minimum gate width you need and photos of backyard jobs. Wins the pool digs and retaining wall jobs bigger outfits can't fit into.

Wet and dry hire info

Builder and developer page

Job galleries by type

Quick quote form

Asks for job type, site address, access width and soil or rock conditions if known, with photo upload. You can price it or plan a visit without guessing.

Questions excavation and earthmoving ask

Straight answers.

How does SEO work for an earthmoving business?

I build pages around searches like site cut excavation, tight access excavator and machine hire, plus your service areas. A fleet page with real machine details helps too, because people search for specific gear. The site is fast, marked up with schema, and your Google Business Profile is set up. Rankings vary by area, but the foundations will be right.

What photos should I send?

Your machines clean and on site, shot side-on so people can judge the size. Then job photos: site cuts, trenches, rock breaking, and before and after on cleared blocks. Tight-access shots squeezing through a gate are great. Drone photos of big jobs look excellent. Daylight phone photos are fine.

Should I list every machine I own?

Yes, if you're happy for clients to book them. I'll set up a fleet page with each machine's size, width and attachments. It answers the most common phone question before it's asked and helps builders book the right gear. When you buy or sell a machine, it's quick to update.

Can the site work for both builders and homeowners?

It can. Homeowners need plain explanations and reassurance about access, mess and lawn damage. Builders want capability, insurance and availability. I'll give each a clear path from the homepage, so nobody has to dig through the wrong information to find what they're after.

Can the site cover demolition as well?

Yes. If demolition is part of your work, it gets its own page explaining what you take on, from small structures to full houses, plus your approach to permits, asbestos checks and clean-up where relevant. I'll write it in plain English based on how you actually run those jobs.

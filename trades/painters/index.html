The problem

Why painters lose jobs online.

01

Everyone looks the same

A painting site with stock photos of rollers tells customers nothing. With no visible difference between you and the next painter, they pick the lowest price and you end up in a race to the bottom.

02

Prep is invisible

The real work is in the prep: sanding, filling, patching and priming. If your site doesn't explain it, customers can't see why your quote is higher than someone who'll slap two coats over the cracks.

03

Trust inside the home

People let painters into their house for days at a time. Without faces, clear details and a professional look, they worry about mess, timing and who's turning up, so they hesitate.

Questions painters ask

Straight answers.

How does SEO work for a painting business?

I write separate pages for interior, exterior, roof and commercial painting, each built around what customers search plus your suburbs, towns or counties. The site is fast and properly marked up, and your Google Business Profile is set up to match. Results depend on your area and competition, but specific pages beat a single list of services.

What photos should I send?

Finished rooms in natural light, exteriors on a clear day, and close-ups of sharp cut lines and glossy trim. Before and after pairs are gold, especially on tired weatherboards or brickwork. A photo of the crew with drop sheets down shows you're careful. Phone photos are fine if they're sharp.

Can I show my painting process on the site?

Yes, and it's one of the best ways to justify your price. I'll write a page walking through prep, protection, painting and clean-up in plain English. Customers who read it understand why you take a little longer and charge a little more than the cheapest quote they got.

Should I list the paint brands I use?

If you use quality paint, yes. Many homeowners trust certain brands, and some search for painters who use them. Listing them builds confidence. If you're an accredited applicator for any brand, I'll mention that as well, as long as the accreditation is current.

What if I also do wallpapering or decorating?

Then it goes on the site. Extra services like wallpaper, feature finishes, timber staining or line marking can each get a page if they're a meaningful part of your work. The standard build includes six pages, and anything beyond that I'll quote before you commit.

The problem

Why tilers lose jobs online.

01

Detail lost in thumbnails

Small, compressed photos hide the very thing that makes you better: the precision. When every tiler's gallery looks alike at thumbnail size, customers can't tell the craftsman from the cowboy.

02

Waterproofing not mentioned

Leaky showers are a homeowner's nightmare. If your site doesn't explain your waterproofing, people worry about water damage, and builders hunting for a reliable wet area tiler scroll straight past you.

03

Specialist work goes unseen

Large format, herringbone, mosaics and natural stone take real skill. Without pages showing that work, customers don't know you do it, and premium jobs go to tilers who show it off.

Questions tilers ask

Straight answers.

How does SEO work for tilers?

I build pages around searches like bathroom tiling, floor tiling and shower waterproofing, plus your service areas. Zoomable galleries are optimised so they load fast without losing detail, which Google rewards. Your Google Business Profile is set up too. Results vary by area and competition, but you'll have pages for the work people actually search for.

What photos should I send?

Straight-on shots of finished walls and floors in good light, then close-ups of grout lines, corners, niches and cuts around taps and drains. Take them before the bathroom fills up with towels and bottles. Sharp phone photos are perfect, and the higher the resolution, the better they'll zoom on the site.

Will high-res photos slow the site down?

No. I resize and compress every image properly and only load the full detail version when someone zooms in. The site stays quick on mobile data, and customers still see every grout line when they want a closer look at your work.

Should I mention waterproofing if I subcontract it?

Yes, just be accurate about it. If you use a licensed waterproofer, the page can explain that and how you coordinate the work. Customers mainly want to know it's done right and who's responsible. I'll write it honestly, based on how you actually handle it.

Can the site bring in builder work too?

It can. A dedicated section for builders and renovators shows your capacity, the project types you handle and how to book you in. Builders look for subbies who seem organised and reliable, and a professional site makes that case before you've even spoken.

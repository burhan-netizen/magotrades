The problem

Why plumbers lose jobs online.

01

Emergency searchers bounce

Someone with a flooded laundry won't wait for a heavy site to load. If the call button isn't obvious on a phone, they're gone in seconds and your competitor gets the job, plus all the follow-up work.

02

One page for everything

A single services page listing drains, hot water and gas tells Google very little. Plumbers with a separate page for each job type show up for far more searches, especially the specific ones that turn into bookings.

03

Missed calls on the tools

You can't answer while you're lying under a sink. If the website has no quick way to leave job details, those people simply ring someone else instead of waiting for your call back.

What you get

What a plumber website needs. All of it, built in.

Emergency call bar

A sticky tap-to-call bar on every phone screen, with your after-hours availability spelled out clearly. No hunting, no menus, just one tap to reach you.

Job-specific service pages

Separate pages for blocked drains, hot water, leak detection, gas fitting and bathroom renovations, each written to match what people search when that particular thing breaks.

Licence and insurance shown

Your licence or registration details and insurance displayed clearly. Customers letting a stranger into their home want proof you're qualified, and it quietly sets you apart from unlicensed operators.

Hot water brands listed

A section listing the hot water systems and brands you install and service. People often search the brand name printed on their tank, and this helps you catch them.

Quick job request form

A short form asking what's wrong, where and how urgent, with the option to attach a photo of the problem. You turn up knowing which parts to bring.

Suburb and town coverage

Clear service area pages so a search for plumber plus their suburb, town or county lands on you. It also cuts the wasted calls from outside your patch.

Questions plumbers ask

Straight answers.

How do you handle SEO for a plumbing business?

Plumbing searches are very local and very specific, so I write a page for each main job and build in your service areas. Pages load fast, the schema marks you as a plumber, and your Google Business Profile is set up to match. Rankings depend on your area and competition, so I won't promise top spot, but you'll have a strong base.

What photos work best for a plumber's website?

Clean finished work looks great: new bathrooms, tidy hot water installs, neat pipework under a sink. Photos of your van with signage and you on the job build trust. Skip the gross blocked drain shots unless they're a clear before and after. Daylight phone photos are completely fine.

Can people book a time through the site?

The standard build includes a tap-to-call button and a quote form. If you want proper online booking with time slots, I'll quote that before you commit. Plenty of plumbers prefer the phone anyway, because you can judge how urgent a job is in a ten second chat.

What happens when I miss calls while I'm working?

The job form catches people who don't want to wait for a call back. If missed calls are a real problem, my AI receptionist service answers when you're on the tools, takes down the job details and sends them straight to you. That's a separate service, quoted on its own.

Do I have to pay monthly for the site?

No. It's a single build fee and you own the domain and the code. The first year of edits and support is included. After that you can leave it alone and pay nothing, pay an hourly rate when you want changes, or update photos and services yourself through a simple CMS.

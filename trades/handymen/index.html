The problem

Why handymen lose jobs online.

01

Too vague to find

Nobody searches for general handyman services. They search for the specific fix they need. A vague site misses those searches and makes it look like you don't do any one thing particularly well.

02

Pricing fears

Customers worry a small job will turn into a big bill. If your site says nothing about how you price, minimum charges or call-out fees, they hesitate and look for someone who explains it clearly.

03

No path for property managers

Landlords and agencies send steady, repeat work, but they need to know you're insured, reliable and able to report back. A site built only for homeowners never gets you onto their list.

Questions handymen ask

Straight answers.

How does SEO work for a handyman business?

I write pages around the specific jobs people search for, like furniture assembly, door repairs or gutter cleaning, combined with your service areas. The site loads fast, uses schema to mark you as a local handyman, and your Google Business Profile is set up properly. Results depend on your area, but specific pages get you found for far more searches.

What photos should I send?

Before and after shots of repairs, fences, decks and paint touch-ups. Photos of you with your van and tools help people feel comfortable letting you in. Even small jobs like a neatly mounted TV or level shelves are worth showing. Daylight phone photos are completely fine, and I'll tidy them up.

Should I put my prices on the website?

You don't need a full price list, but explaining how you charge helps a lot. Your hourly approach, a minimum call-out and how you quote bigger jobs is usually enough. It sets expectations early and puts off people hunting for something unrealistically cheap.

Can I target landlords and property managers?

Yes. I'll build a section aimed at them, covering insurance, how you report jobs with photos and how quickly you can get to a tenant issue. That kind of client sends repeat work month after month, so it's worth having a page that speaks directly to them.

I already get work by word of mouth. Why bother?

Word of mouth is great, but people who've been referred to you still look you up before calling. A solid site confirms you're legit and makes it easy to get in touch. It also brings in new customers searching Google, so you're not relying on referrals alone.

The problem

Why landscapers lose jobs online.

01

The transformation is missing

The magic of landscaping is the change. Without before and after photos side by side, customers see a nice garden but not what you did, so your work looks easier and cheaper than it really was.

02

Mowing calls instead of projects

When your site doesn't separate maintenance from design and construct, you get buried in small mowing enquiries. The big backyard makeovers go to landscapers who clearly show that's what they do.

03

Seasonal ups and downs

Spring brings a rush and winter brings silence. Without content that sells cooler-month work like retaining walls, drainage or hardscaping, your diary empties out exactly when you need steady jobs.

Questions landscapers ask

Straight answers.

How does SEO work for landscapers?

I target the searches people make when planning a project, like retaining wall builder or backyard landscaping, along with your service areas. The site is fast, marked up with schema and connected to a properly set up Google Business Profile. Results depend on your area and competition, but a page for each service gives you many more chances to show up.

What photos should I send?

Before and after pairs taken from the same spot are the most valuable thing you can send. Then finished gardens in soft morning or late afternoon light, close-ups of paving and stonework, and lighting shots at dusk. Drone photos of bigger yards look fantastic. Sharp phone photos are absolutely fine.

Can I stop getting so many mowing enquiries?

Yes. I'll design the site around the work you want most, with big projects up front and maintenance clearly separate, or left off entirely. The enquiry form can ask for budget and project type, so small jobs filter themselves out before they reach your phone.

Do I need separate sites for maintenance and design?

No. One site with two clear sections works better for Google and for customers. Each section has its own pages, photos and contact path, so a homeowner after a full makeover never has to wade through mowing prices, and the regular lawn customer finds their bit quickly.

Can I update the photos myself?

During the first year I handle edits for you as part of support. After that you can use a light CMS to add new photos, reviews and services yourself for free, or pay an hourly rate and send them to me. Your Instagram feed keeps updating the site automatically either way.

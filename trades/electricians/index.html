The problem

Why electricians lose jobs online.

01

Licence not visible

Customers are told to check that an electrician is licensed. If they can't find your number quickly, they assume the worst or move on, especially for bigger jobs like rewires and switchboard replacements.

02

Missing the growth work

EV chargers, solar and home batteries are where demand is heading. Without pages that target those searches, homeowners doing their research find installers who explain the options, and you never get a look in.

03

Looks like every other sparky

Generic sites with stock lightbulb photos make every electrician look identical. When nothing stands out, customers pick on price, and you end up competing with the cheapest bloke on the local forum.

What you get

What a electrician website needs. All of it, built in.

Licence number on every page

Your licence or registration number, insurance and any accreditations shown in the header or footer. It's small, but it answers the first question careful customers ask.

Emergency call-out section

A clear section for power outages, tripping safety switches and sparking outlets, with a tap-to-call button and after-hours details. Built for the person standing in a dark kitchen.

EV charger page

A page covering home EV charger installs: the charger types you fit, what a switchboard check involves and the brands you work with. Catches buyers early in their research.

Solar and battery info

Plain-English explanations of the solar and battery systems you install, with photos of your real installs. Helps you win considered, higher-value jobs from people comparing quotes.

Commercial and residential split

If you do both, each gets its own section. Property managers and shop owners see commercial work and maintenance contracts, while homeowners see renovations and safety checks.

Project photos that make sense

Neat switchboards, lighting designs and finished installs, captioned so customers understand what they're looking at. Tidy work photographed well says more than any sales line.

Questions electricians ask

Straight answers.

How do you approach SEO for electricians?

I build pages around the searches that bring in paying work, like emergency electrician, switchboard upgrade or EV charger installation, plus your service areas. Fast loading, schema that marks you as an electrician and a properly set up Google Business Profile all help. I can't promise rankings, since that depends on your area, but the groundwork will be solid.

What photos should I send?

Your neatest switchboards, finished lighting jobs, EV chargers on garage walls and solar panels on roofs. A photo of you or your team in uniform next to the van helps people trust you. Sharp phone photos in good light are fine. If you've got before and after shots of messy old wiring, send those too.

Should I show my licence number online?

Yes. Careful customers look for it, and in many places they're actively encouraged to check it. Putting it on every page makes you look established and removes a reason to hesitate. I'll also list your insurance and any manufacturer accreditations you currently hold.

Can the site help me get more solar and EV work?

It gives you a real shot at it. Those customers research before they call, so detailed pages that explain the options and show your installs make a difference. I write them in plain English and target the searches people make while comparing installers. Results vary, but you can't win those jobs without the pages.

Who writes the words on the site?

I do. Every word is written for you, based on a chat about your work, your areas and the jobs you want more of. You read it, tell me what's off, and I fix it. No templates and no filler lifted from other electricians' websites.

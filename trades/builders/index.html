The problem

Why builders lose jobs online.

01

Projects with no story

A grid of finished photos doesn't tell a client how you handled the tricky slope, the council approval or the tight budget. Without case studies, your best work looks like everybody else's best work.

02

The process is a mystery

Big projects scare people. If your site doesn't explain how you quote, schedule, communicate and handle variations, clients assume the worst and lean towards builders who make it all feel manageable.

03

Wasted quoting evenings

Without clear info on the work you take on, you end up driving out to quote tiny jobs or projects outside your range, losing evenings to meetings that were never going to go anywhere.

What you get

What a builder website needs. All of it, built in.

Project case studies

Real projects with before, during and after photos, plus the brief, the challenges and the result. Gives clients proof you can handle a job like theirs, and gives Google rich content to rank.

How we work page

Your process from first meeting to handover: design, quoting, approvals, build stages and defects. Takes the fear out of a big decision and screens out clients who aren't a fit.

Licences and insurance

Builder licence or registration, insurances and memberships shown clearly. Clients spending big check these, and visible credentials put you ahead of builders who make them dig.

Pages by project type

Separate pages for extensions, renovations, new homes, kitchens and bathrooms, or whatever you focus on. Each targets the searches clients make when planning that kind of project.

Detailed enquiry form

Asks for project type, location, rough budget range and timeline. You can tell straight away whether a site meeting is worth booking, which saves everyone time.

Areas you build in

Clear pages for the suburbs, towns or counties you work in, so local searches find you and people outside your range know before they pick up the phone.

Questions builders ask

Straight answers.

How does SEO help a building company?

Clients search phrases like house extension builder or renovation builder plus their area. I build pages around those, write project case studies Google can read, and set up your Google Business Profile properly. Builder searches can be competitive, so results vary, but a fast site with real content gives you a fair shot against bigger firms.

What photos should I send?

Finished projects shot in good light, inside and out, plus progress photos from frame stage through to handover. Before photos are valuable for renovations. If you've had professional photography done, send that first. A shot of you on site with the team helps clients put a face to the business.

Can I show projects in detail?

Yes, and I'd recommend it. Each case study can cover the brief, the challenges, the materials and the result, with its own gallery. Three or four strong case studies do more for you than fifty random photos. I write them from a quick chat with you about each job.

Will the site cut down on time-wasters?

It helps a lot. Clear information about the projects you take on, plus a form that asks for budget range and timeline, means people filter themselves. You'll still get the odd tyre kicker, but far fewer evenings spent quoting jobs that were never right for you.

Do I own the website?

Yes, completely. You own the domain and the code, with no monthly rent and no lock-in. The first year of edits and support is included. After that you can leave it, pay hourly for changes, or add new photos and projects yourself through a simple CMS at no cost.

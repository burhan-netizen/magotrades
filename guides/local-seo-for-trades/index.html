How do tradies and contractors get found on Google Maps?

Google ranks local businesses on three things: relevance (how well your profile matches the search), distance (how close you are to the searcher) and prominence (how well known and well reviewed you are). You get found by setting up a complete, verified Google Business Profile with the right category, earning a steady flow of genuine reviews, and backing it up with a website that has a page for each service and area you cover.

How do tradies get on the Google Maps 3-pack?

You get into the 3-pack (the map and three businesses shown at the top of local searches) by being the most relevant, close and trusted option for that search. Google says this itself: local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and there is no way to pay for a better local ranking.

Relevance is about whether Google understands what you do. Distance is about where the searcher is standing, which you cannot control. Prominence is about how well known you are, measured through things like reviews, your rating, and other websites mentioning and linking to you.

So the job is simple to describe and slow to do: tell Google clearly what you do and where, then build proof that real customers use you. Nobody can honestly promise you a spot in the 3-pack, because the results change with every searcher's location.

What matters most for ranking in Google Maps?

Your primary Google Business Profile category matters more than anything else you control. In Whitespark's 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors survey of local SEO specialists, primary category was rated the number one factor for the local pack, ahead of proximity to the searcher.

The same survey put these near the top: being open at the time of the search, a high Google star rating, the number of Google reviews with written text, your additional categories, and accurate placement of your map pin. Review signals were rated more important than in previous years, including how recent your reviews are.

Keywords in the business name also scored highly. That is not a reason to add "Best Plumber Sydney" to your name. Google's guidelines ban extra keywords in the name and it is a common reason trade profiles get suspended. Use your real, registered trading name.

Why is my business not showing on Google Maps?

The most common reasons are that the profile is not verified, the category is wrong, or you are being searched for from too far away. Check these first before spending money on anything else.

Other causes are worth ruling out too:

  • The profile is unverified, suspended, or still has edits under review.
  • Your primary category describes something you do occasionally rather than your main trade.
  • You are searching from your own home, where Google already knows you; ask someone across town to search instead.
  • Your service areas are vague or far wider than you really work.
  • You have very few reviews compared with the businesses that do show.
  • Your name, address or phone number differs between your website, your profile and directories.
  • You are outside business hours and set as closed, since being open at the time of search is a ranking factor.

Do I need a Google Business Profile if I work from home or my ute?

Yes. Most tradespeople should set up as a service-area business, which lets you have a full profile without showing your home address. Google defines a service-area business as one that visits or delivers to customers rather than serving them at its address, and plumbers are one of its own examples.

You list up to 20 service areas by suburb, city or postcode, and Google asks you to keep them within about two hours' drive of your base. You cannot set a radius. Be honest here: listing every town in the state does not help you rank in places you are far from, and it can look suspicious.

One trade-off is worth knowing. Specialists rate a visible address in the searched city as a strong ranking signal, so a business with a real, signed workshop or office has an edge in that town. Never invent one. Virtual offices and mailbox addresses are banned and are a fast route to suspension.

How many reviews does a trade business need?

There is no magic number; you need more recent, genuine reviews than the businesses you compete with in your area. A roofer in a small town might do well with 30. A plumber in a capital city may be up against competitors with several hundred.

Recency matters to customers as much as to Google. BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey of US consumers found 74% look for reviews from the last three months, 68% want at least a 4 star rating, and 89% expect owners to reply to reviews.

The easiest system is to ask at the moment the customer is happiest: when the job is finished and they have seen the result. Send a direct link by text that same day. Never pay or offer discounts for reviews and never filter out unhappy customers. Google's policies ban both, and consumer law in several of these countries does too.

Does my website affect my Google Maps ranking?

Yes, but mostly indirectly. Your website helps Google confirm what you do and where, and it is the page most people check before they call. It matters even more for the normal organic results that sit under the map.

In the 2026 Whitespark survey, a dedicated page for each service was rated the top factor for local organic rankings. That means a separate page for "hot water system repairs" and another for "blocked drains", not one page that lists everything.

Service-area pages help too, if they are real. A page for each main town you cover, with jobs you have done there, local photos and specific details, is useful. Fifty copies of the same page with the suburb name swapped are not, and Google treats that kind of thin content as low value.

What is NAP consistency and does it still matter?

NAP stands for name, address and phone number, and keeping them identical everywhere helps Google trust that all those listings are the same business. It is basic hygiene rather than a ranking booster these days, but mismatches cause confusion and can split your reviews across duplicate listings.

Pick one exact version of your business name and one phone number, ideally a local or mobile number you control, and use it on your website, Google profile, invoices, vans and every directory. If you move or change numbers, update everything, not just Google.

Which directories should tradies be listed on?

Get listed on the big general platforms plus the trade directories customers actually use in your country. Specialists rate citations as a smaller factor for the map pack than they used to be, but they matter for being trusted and for AI tools, which read these sites.

Start with Bing Places for Business, Apple Business Connect and Facebook in every country. Then add the ones that fit where you work:

  • Australia: hipages, Oneflare, ServiceSeeking, Yellow Pages and True Local.
  • United Kingdom: Checkatrade, TrustATrader, MyBuilder and Yell.
  • United States: Angi, Yelp, BBB, Nextdoor and Houzz (Houzz suits builders, remodelers and landscapers most).
  • Canada: HomeStars, Yelp and your local chamber of commerce.
  • New Zealand: Builderscrack, NoCowboys and Yellow NZ.
  • Your trade association, licensing body and supplier "find an installer" pages, which are often the most trusted mentions you can get.

Do photos on Google Business Profile help?

Photos help people choose you, and Google lists adding photos as one of its own tips for improving local presence. Real job photos beat stock images every time, because customers can tell the difference.

Upload a few new photos every month: finished work, before and after shots, your van with signage, your team on site. Take them on your phone at the job. That steady activity also shows Google, and customers, that the business is active.

How do I get my trade business recommended by ChatGPT and other AI assistants?

AI assistants recommend businesses they can find clear, consistent information about across the web, so the same work that helps on Google helps here. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 45% of US consumers now use ChatGPT or similar tools for local recommendations, up from 6% the year before.

ChatGPT's search relies heavily on Bing, so claim Bing Places. Studies of its sources show it leans on business websites, directories, review sites and local mentions. Whitespark's 2026 report found three of the top five AI visibility factors were citation factors.

In practice: keep your listings consistent, make your website spell out your services, areas, licences and prices or price ranges in plain words, add an FAQ that answers real customer questions, and earn mentions on local sites. Inside Google Maps, an AI "Ask" feature has replaced the old Q&A section in some countries, and it draws its answers from your profile, reviews and website.

How long does local SEO take for a trade business?

Expect a few weeks for profile fixes to show and several months for reviews and website work to build up. Fixing a wrong category can move things quickly. Climbing past established competitors with hundreds of reviews takes steady effort over months.

Treat it like maintenance on your van: a little every week. Ask for reviews after every job, add photos monthly, reply to every review, and keep your details accurate. Your checklist.

  • Claim and verify your Google Business Profile
  • Set your primary category to your main trade
  • Use your real business name with no extra keywords
  • Clear your home address and list up to 20 real service areas
  • Text a review link to every happy customer the day the job is done
  • Reply to every review, good and bad
  • Add a separate website page for each main service
  • Claim Bing Places and Apple Business Connect
  • List on two or three trade directories used in your country
  • Upload new job photos every month

Quick answers

Common questions.

Can I pay Google to rank higher on Google Maps?

No. Google states there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Ads such as Local Services Ads appear separately and are labelled as sponsored.

Should I show my home address on Google?

No. If customers do not visit you, set up as a service-area business and remove the address so it is not shown. Google asks for this.

Can I use a virtual office to rank in a nearby city?

No. Google's guidelines say virtual offices and mailbox addresses are not eligible, and using one is a common cause of suspension.

Do I need a website if I have a Google Business Profile?

You can get calls without one, but a website gives Google and customers far more proof of what you do. It also lets you rank in the normal results and gives AI assistants something to read.

Why do competitors with fewer reviews rank above me?

Usually because they are closer to the searcher, have a better matched category, or have a real address in that town. Reviews are one factor among several.

Is local SEO different in Australia, the UK, the US, Canada and New Zealand?

The Google side works the same way in all five. The main differences are which trade directories and review sites customers use in each country.

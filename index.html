Three things a website has to do, in this order

Be found. Be trusted. Be chosen.

01 / SEO AND GOOGLE

Found.

Nobody can hire the contractor they never saw. Tonight someone in your area is searching for exactly what you do. Either your name comes up, or somebody else's does.

  • Pages written for your trade and every area you cover
  • Google Business Profile set up and wired to the site
  • Loads fast on a phone with two bars of signal
  • Clean structure Google can actually read
02 / TRUST AND CREDIBILITY

Trusted.

They have never met you. They give you about eight seconds to prove you are the real thing. So we stop telling and start showing.

  • Your real jobs, your real crew. No stock photos, ever
  • Before and after sliders they can drag themselves
  • Reviews, credentials and guarantees right up front
  • Copy that sounds like you on a good day
03 / CALLS AND QUOTES

Chosen.

Trust is nice. A phone call is better. Every screen has one job: make it stupidly easy to say "come and take a look".

  • Tap to call from every screen
  • Quote forms that take a minute and text you the job
  • Calculators and tools that get them halfway to yes
  • Built for a thumb, on a job site, in full sun

Why I do this

I have never learned how to build a small project like a small project.

01 / The day job

I spent eight years inside banks, fintech and insurance. Big buildings, big numbers. And the one thing I carried out with me was this: people do not buy the best. They buy the one they trust first.

02 / The nights

For two of those years I built websites at night, after the day job, because I could not leave it alone. Then I stopped calling it a side thing and started Mago Labs.

03 / You

Here is what I know about you. You are good. Seriously good. Your work is poured, laid, dug and fixed all over town, and it will still be standing long after the invoice is forgotten. The people who have seen it swear by you. But the people who have not are searching right now, and they are finding somebody with half your skill and a better website.

04 / Why it bothers me

That bothers me more than it should. I get attached. So I build every site as if it were my own name on the side of the truck. And when your phone rings with a stranger who already trusts you, that is my win. When you tell a friend who built it, that is the only referral I am after.
